## Factual and interpretive decisions

- Early Christianity: Romans 16 supports recommendations and household gatherings, not a centrally planned route to statehood. Gregory's letter and Carole Straw's scholarly account support the later administrative comparison. Straw's publisher PDF was read visually at printed/PDF page 5.
- Papal territory: Delogu's Treccani biography distinguishes the contested 754 promise and lost 756 donation instrument. Frankish armed intervention is explicit. The Holy See, Papal States, and Vatican City are distinguished.
- Hanse: modern heritage overviews supply institutional context; Stralsund's city archivist supplies the treaty narrative. The anti-Danish coalition included non-Hanseatic towns and princely allies. Commercial success is not called incomplete statehood. Bruges is not described as a walled sovereign enclave.
- Venice: Orseolo was already doge; the date around 1000 is qualified. The 1204 sack and much later defensive works are not collapsed into one event or one unchanged map.
- Reformation: printed indulgences show established institutions also using print. Augsburg's text limits the confessions covered and places emigration alongside its material obligations. No claim of universal religious freedom.
- LDS: migration, settlement, federal territorial authority, and U.S. statehood are separate. Lewis's account makes Indigenous habitation, resource conflict, and dispossession explicit; it is not generalized to every Indigenous people.
- Yishuv: UNSCOP chapter II supports pre-state institutions and competing claims. The report's Arab-boycott limitation is noted. Declaration, recognition, war, and Palestinian displacement remain separate factual claims. No inference that institutional competence establishes a right to displace others.
- Owen/Fourier: the examples concern intentional communities, not literal sovereign-state campaigns. The North American Phalanx is not treated as Owen's project. The NPS source's broad characterizations of government and its fire chronology are not repeated.

- British East India Company: distinguish the English charter of 1600, commercial factors, Plassey's Indian soldiers and allies, the 1765 diwani, parliamentary supervision, the end of trade in 1833, the transfer of government in 1858, and corporate dissolution in 1874. The British Library, National Army Museum, and UK Parliament supply the relevant passages; the article does not treat company rule as independent sovereignty.
- Dutch VOC: the Nationaal Archief describes powers delegated by the States General in 1602. The Rijksmuseum supplies Batavia's foundation in 1619 after Jakarta's destruction. The Nationaal Archief's Banda account and reproduced May 6, 1621 letter document coercion behind the monopoly; no casualty estimate is repeated. Company authority is not projected uniformly across present-day national borders.
- Citations: meaningful short phrases within sentences link to stable local reference anchors; the reference entries hold the external URLs and passage locators.

## Historical expansion, September 17, 2026

The English revision uses en-US prose. Each case now opens with its period and geography, establishes the preceding setting, and follows early institutions into mature power and later decline, absorption, or dissolution. The Reformation remains contextual material, not a newly founded state.

- Papacy: added the Italian setting, medieval consolidation, Albornoz, Julius II’s military diplomacy, central and provincial government, and the nineteenth-century territorial losses. The Catholic Encyclopedia is labeled as a dated confessional source; its normative judgments are not adopted. Treccani and the linked Wikipedia overview provide cross-checks and administrative context.
- Overseas authority: the May 4, 1493 Inter caetera and June 7, 1494 Treaty of Tordesillas are distinct. The papal 100-league demarcation and the crowns’ negotiated 370-league line are not conflated. The treaty text verifies the parties, assignment, and joint survey provisions; NPS supports the consequence for Brazil’s coast. Modern national boundaries and Indigenous consent are not inferred from European grants.
- Hanse and Venice: added the northern trading region, the lagoon/Byzantine background, later institutional limits, the 1669 final Hanseatic assembly without formal dissolution, and Venice’s 1797 end.
- Companies: added commercial context and stages of military/fiscal government; the VOC’s 1799 dissolution and transfer of possessions and debt are distinguished from the end of colonial rule.
- Migration and communities: added the LDS route and federal/statehood settlement, the Mandate background and immediate 1948 outcome, New Harmony’s predecessor settlement, and the Phalanx’s working organization and liquidation. Duration is no longer offered as evidence that one compensation policy caused survival.
- Author motivation: the user supplied the digital-trust and jurisdiction connection. The existing manifesto source was read and its published route returned HTTP 200 with the expected title. ERC-5485’s official text confirms authorship, Review status, the jurisdiction/accreditation distinction, and enforcement-proposal semantics.
