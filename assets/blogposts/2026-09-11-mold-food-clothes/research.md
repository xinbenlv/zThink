## Deliberate boundaries

- US public-agency household guidance is identified as such. No blanket cutting rule: 1 inch around AND below, knife kept out of mold, new wrap for hard cheese, small spots only for firm produce, all sliced/shredded/crumbled cheese discarded. Specific shelf-stable meat exceptions are not generalized to Chinese cured meat.
- Toxic compounds are distinguished from the organism. Freezing only supports microbial inactivation/reactivation, not toxin removal. No claim that all toxins survive every possible industrial process.
- No fabricated personal anecdote or diagnosis; no inference from color to toxicity; no extrapolation from foodborne aflatoxin cancer evidence to incidental household odor exposure.
- UGA’s older stain-removal recipes (including solvents and lemon/bleach alternatives) were not adopted. The newer C1047-2 clothing row was read. Care labels and the whole garment govern material compatibility. No universal bleach concentration, duration or temperature is published.
- Moisture guidance differs: EPA below 60%, ideally 30–50%; CDC at most 50% all day. USDA’s food page also mentions below 40%; the article deliberately attributes its indoor guidance to EPA/CDC rather than mixing the numbers.
- 24–48 hours is a drying/prevention target, and 10 ft² is a remediation-scale reference, neither a diagnostic threshold. Flood/sewage cleanup is not equated with ordinary closet laundry.
- No commercial cleaning supplier or remediation business is used as health evidence.
